How EU directives become national law, country by country

An EU directive sets goals; each of the 27 member states writes its own law to reach them. OpenAcquis is an open project to map that process provision by provision: which national section implements which EU article, where a country left something out, and where it added extra rules.

Status: v1.0 preview (v0.5). For every directive below, the site lists the national measures each member state has notified to the European Commission (EU Publications Office, CELLAR). For the twelve directives of the registry it also goes down to the provision in Czechia, Slovakia, Austria and Germany. Everything at provision level is an unreviewed machine suggestion, shown next to the government's official correlation table where one exists (seven tables in Czechia and Slovakia so far). Nothing on this site says whether a country transposed a directive correctly; see the roadmap.
Provision by provision: open a directive, or use the country links on each card (✓ = official correlation table shown alongside, * = compared with existing law: the act aligned was not notified for the directive, – = no act to align). Start with the NIS2 article × country matrix. You can help: review suggestions. Open data: links.csv · links.jsonl · datapackage.json (CC-BY-4.0), the static API, and an Atom feed of changes to the directives and national acts watched.

    How it works

    1. Which national acts live

      Member states notify the Commission of the measures they adopt for each directive. We read these records from CELLAR, the database behind EUR-Lex, and keep a registry of the main act per country.

    2. Texts CZ, SK, AT, DE

      The directive in each official language, and consolidated national laws from national portals (e-Sbírka, Slov-Lex, RIS, gesetze-im-internet.de), split into articles, paragraphs and points. In the command-line tool and side by side on the alignment pages.

    3. Matching 12 directives × 4 countries

      Text similarity (lexical + BM25 fusion, optionally with a multilingual model) proposes which national provision implements which EU provision. It is scored against seven official correlation tables from Czechia and Slovakia; six of them were not used for tuning. Leftovers become gap and extra-rule candidates.

    4. Human review open

      Suggestions stay labelled as suggestions until a person checks them on a review page. Official correlation tables and reviewed links form an open CC-BY dataset and a static API (data & API).

    Why

    The EU records which national laws a country says implement a directive, but nobody publishes which section implements which article. For NIS2, member states notified hundreds of national measures; Czechia alone notified 83. Finding which one implements a given article means reading them all. That knowledge is sold by law firms one directive at a time; OpenAcquis aims to make it an open public good.

    Want to help? Country adapters, legal review and evaluation data are all welcome: see CONTRIBUTING.

    Not legal advice. OpenAcquis is a research and transparency tool. Notified measures are what member states report; they can be incomplete or include broadly related acts. Always check the official texts.
