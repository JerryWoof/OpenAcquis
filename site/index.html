How EU directives become national law, country by country

An EU directive sets goals; each of the 27 member states writes its own law to reach them. OpenAcquis is an open project to map that process provision by provision: which national section implements which EU article, where a country left something out, and where it added extra rules.

Status: early prototype (v0.4). For ten directives this site shows act-level data: the national measures each member state has notified to the European Commission as transposing a directive, taken from the EU Publications Office (CELLAR). For NIS2 it goes down to the provision in four countries. Everything at provision level is a machine suggestion until a person reviews it; see the roadmap.
New: NIS2 in four countries. Article × country matrix for Czechia, Slovakia, Austria and Germany, and provision-by-provision pages: Czechia (next to the government's official correlation table), Slovakia, Austria, Germany, each with gap and extra-rule candidates. You can help: review suggestions. Download the open dataset: links.csv · links.jsonl · datapackage.json (CC-BY-4.0).

    How it works

    1. Which national acts live

      Member states notify the Commission of the measures they adopt for each directive. We read these records from CELLAR, the database behind EUR-Lex.

    2. Texts v0.2–0.4 · CZ, SK, AT, DE

      The directive in each official language, and consolidated national laws from national portals (e-Sbírka, Slov-Lex, RIS, gesetze-im-internet.de), split into articles, paragraphs and points. In the command-line tool and side by side on the NIS2 pages.

    3. Matching v0.4 · NIS2 × 4 countries

      Text similarity (lexical + BM25 fusion, optionally with a multilingual model) proposes which national provision implements which EU provision, scored against the official Czech correlation table. Leftovers become gap and extra-rule candidates. See the NIS2 matrix.

    4. Human review v0.4

      Suggestions stay labelled as suggestions until a person checks them on the review page. Reviewed links and official correlation tables form an open CC-BY dataset (download).

    Why

    The EU records which national laws a country says implement a directive, but nobody publishes which section implements which article. For NIS2, member states notified hundreds of national measures; Czechia alone notified 83. Finding which one implements a given article means reading them all. That knowledge is sold by law firms one directive at a time; OpenAcquis aims to make it an open public good.

    Want to help? Country adapters, legal review and evaluation data are all welcome: see CONTRIBUTING.

    Not legal advice. OpenAcquis is a research and transparency tool. Notified measures are what member states report; they can be incomplete or include broadly related acts. Always check the official texts.
