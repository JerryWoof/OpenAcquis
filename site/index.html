How EU directives become national law, country by country

An EU directive sets goals; each of the 27 member states writes its own law to reach them. OpenAcquis is an open project to map that process provision by provision: which national section implements which EU article, where a country left something out, and where it added extra rules.

Status: early prototype. This site shows act-level data that works today: the national measures each member state has notified to the European Commission as transposing a directive, taken from the EU Publications Office (CELLAR). Provision-level matching (article × country, with side-by-side texts) is on the roadmap.
New: first provision-level map. NIS2 × Czechia, provision by provision: every NIS2 paragraph next to the Czech provisions the government's official correlation table lists for it and the top machine suggestion, with both texts side by side. Machine suggestions are unreviewed.

    How it works

    1. Which national acts live

      Member states notify the Commission of the measures they adopt for each directive. We read these records from CELLAR, the database behind EUR-Lex.

    2. Texts v0.2–0.3 · CLI + NIS2 × CZ

      The directive in each official language, and consolidated national laws from national portals, split into articles, paragraphs and points. Available in the command-line tool for EU directives (24 languages) and Czech law (e-Sbírka); shown on this site side by side for NIS2 × Czechia.

    3. Matching v0.3 · NIS2 × CZ

      Text similarity proposes which national provision implements which EU provision, scored against the official Czech correlation table; an AI judge can label each pair (same, partial, different value, stricter, option chosen). First result: NIS2 × Czechia.

    4. Human review v0.4

      Suggestions stay labelled as suggestions until a person checks them. Reviewed results become an open CC-BY dataset.

    Why

    The EU records which national laws a country says implement a directive, but nobody publishes which section implements which article. For NIS2, member states notified hundreds of national measures; Czechia alone notified 83. Finding which one implements a given article means reading them all. That knowledge is sold by law firms one directive at a time; OpenAcquis aims to make it an open public good.

    Want to help? Country adapters, legal review and evaluation data are all welcome: see CONTRIBUTING.

    Not legal advice. OpenAcquis is a research and transparency tool. Notified measures are what member states report; they can be incomplete or include broadly related acts. Always check the official texts.
