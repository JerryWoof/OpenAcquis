Country comparison

Austria, Czechia, Germany and Slovakia side by side: for how many EU provisions an AI judge found a related national provision among the machine's candidates, and where it saw a national text that looks stricter or sets a different value. Machine-estimated and unreviewed; countries are listed alphabetically, not ranked.

Read this first: how these numbers are made.
  • An AI judge, not a lawyer. Every label comes from Claude (Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5, depending on the pair). Sonnet labels more candidates “partial” (see Opus vs Sonnet), so the lists of differences below only compare cells judged by the same model. Nobody has reviewed these labels.
  • It only sees three candidates per paragraph. The judge reads the top three national provisions that the text matcher proposes for each directive paragraph. “Machine-estimated coverage” is the share of paragraphs for which at least one of those three was judged related. A low figure often means the matcher missed the right section, not that a country has no provision; the same limit applies to every country.
  • Measured precision. Precision was measured on 7 of the 46 pairs, measured on 2026-11-15: all Czech or Slovak, the ones with an official correlation table, all shown here with Opus labels. On those, the judge's related labels match a link of the table about 74–79 % of the time (0.79 for Opus; a Sonnet re-run of the same 7 pairs gave 0.74; the tables are not exhaustive). The other 39 pairs, in all four countries, are unmeasured. Details: docs/evaluation.md. Texts are in Czech, Slovak or German, and the judge reads each in its own language.
  • Not a ranking. The figures do not rank countries and say nothing about how any country implemented EU law. They depend on the matcher, the language, how a national law is structured (one act or many) and the judge model. Totals pool paragraphs, so large directives weigh more, and countries have different numbers of directives aligned.
  • Pairs whose national act was not notified for the directive (“not notified”) are compared as existing law: they are shown in the matrix but left out of the totals and of the lists below.

    Directives by country: machine-estimated coverage and breakdown of the AI judge's labels

    What looks specific to each country

    Judgments of “stricter” or “different value”, highest AI confidence first, quotes verified in both texts, at most two per directive. They are candidates for a closer look: the judge may have compared the wrong provisions, and a difference is not a finding. Also shown: the directives where candidates were found more or less often than in the other countries (average of the other countries with a cell of at least 20 paragraphs judged by the same model, at least two of them), listed only when the difference is at least 5 percentage points.

    Not legal advice. Numbers on this page are unreviewed, machine-estimated differences between candidate provisions. They are not findings about how any country implemented a directive, and countries are listed alphabetically, not ranked. Always check the official texts.
